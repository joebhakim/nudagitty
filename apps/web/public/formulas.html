Copula formulas

0 · Setup

A copula is a joint CDF on \([0,1]^d\) with uniform margins. Sklar: any joint \(F\) factors as \[ F(x_1,\dots,x_d) = C\big(F_1(x_1),\dots,F_d(x_d)\big), \] with \(C\) unique on \(\prod \mathrm{Ran}(F_k)\). Write \(u_k=F_k(x_k)\) (the probability-integral transform — "rank space").

The workhorse for conditioning and simulation is the h-function (a conditional CDF): \[ h(u\mid v) \;=\; \frac{\partial C(u,v)}{\partial v} \;=\; \Pr(U\le u \mid V=v), \qquad h^{-1}(w\mid v)\ \text{its inverse in the first argument.} \]

Rank dependence measures, invariant to the margins: \[ \tau = 4\!\int_{[0,1]^2}\! C\,dC - 1, \qquad \rho_S = 12\!\int_{[0,1]^2}\! uv\,dC(u,v) - 3. \] Tail dependence (limiting co-exceedance): \[ \lambda_L=\lim_{q\to 0^+}\Pr(U\le q\mid V\le q),\qquad \lambda_U=\lim_{q\to 1^-}\Pr(U> q\mid V> q). \] Fréchet–Hoeffding bounds: \(\;W(u,v)=\max(u+v-1,0)\ \le\ C\ \le\ M(u,v)=\min(u,v)\), with \(\tau(M)=1\) (comonotone), \(\tau(W)=-1\) (countermonotone), \(\tau(\Pi)=0\) for \(\Pi(u,v)=uv\).

1 · Elliptical copulas

Gaussian

Let \(x=\Phi^{-1}(u),\ y=\Phi^{-1}(v)\), correlation \(\rho\in(-1,1)\).

\[ C(u,v)=\Phi_2(x,y;\rho), \qquad c(u,v)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-\rho^2}}\exp\!\left\{-\frac{\rho^2(x^2+y^2)-2\rho xy}{2(1-\rho^2)}\right\}. \] \[ h(u\mid v)=\Phi\!\left(\frac{x-\rho y}{\sqrt{1-\rho^2}}\right),\qquad h^{-1}(w\mid v)=\Phi\!\left(\rho\,\Phi^{-1}(v)+\sqrt{1-\rho^2}\;\Phi^{-1}(w)\right). \] \[ \tau=\tfrac{2}{\pi}\arcsin\rho,\qquad \rho_S=\tfrac{6}{\pi}\arcsin\tfrac{\rho}{2},\qquad \lambda_L=\lambda_U=0. \]

\(d\)-variate: \(C(\mathbf u;R)=\Phi_d(\Phi^{-1}(u_1),\dots;R)\) for a correlation matrix \(R\). Sample \(\mathbf z\sim\mathcal N(0,R)\) via Cholesky, set \(u_k=\Phi(z_k)\). The full-\(\Sigma\) case people want for many covariates is exactly an all-Gaussian vine (§4).

Student-t

\(x=t_\nu^{-1}(u),\ y=t_\nu^{-1}(v)\), correlation \(\rho\), dof \(\nu\).

\[ C(u,v)=t_{2}(x,y;\rho,\nu),\qquad c(u,v)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-\rho^2}}\cdot\frac{\Gamma\!\big(\tfrac{\nu+2}{2}\big)\,\Gamma\!\big(\tfrac{\nu}{2}\big)}{\Gamma\!\big(\tfrac{\nu+1}{2}\big)^2}\cdot\frac{\big(1+\tfrac{x^2+y^2-2\rho xy}{\nu(1-\rho^2)}\big)^{-\frac{\nu+2}{2}}}{\prod_{s\in\{x,y\}}\big(1+\tfrac{s^2}{\nu}\big)^{-\frac{\nu+1}{2}}}. \] \[ h(u\mid v)=t_{\nu+1}\!\left(\frac{x-\rho y}{\sqrt{\dfrac{(\nu+y^2)(1-\rho^2)}{\nu+1}}}\right),\qquad \tau=\tfrac{2}{\pi}\arcsin\rho. \] \[ \lambda_L=\lambda_U=2\,t_{\nu+1}\!\left(-\sqrt{\frac{(\nu+1)(1-\rho)}{1+\rho}}\right)\;>\;0. \]

Same \(\tau\)–\(\rho\) map as Gaussian, but symmetric tail dependence that vanishes only as \(\nu\to\infty\) — the reason t beats Gaussian for joint extremes.

2 · Archimedean copulas

Built from a generator \(\varphi:[0,1]\to[0,\infty]\), continuous, strictly decreasing, convex, \(\varphi(1)=0\): \[ C(u,v)=\varphi^{[-1]}\!\big(\varphi(u)+\varphi(v)\big),\qquad h(u\mid v)=\frac{\varphi'(v)}{\varphi'\!\big(C(u,v)\big)},\qquad \tau=1+4\!\int_0^1\!\frac{\varphi(t)}{\varphi'(t)}\,dt. \] Bivariate density \(c(u,v)=-\dfrac{\varphi''(C)\,\varphi'(u)\,\varphi'(v)}{\varphi'(C)^3}\). Single-parameter Archimedeans are exchangeable — all pairs identical — which is why high-dimensional dependence needs vines, not one Archimedean.

Clayton — lower tail

\(\varphi(t)=\tfrac{1}{\theta}(t^{-\theta}-1),\ \theta>0\) (extends to \(\theta\ge-1\)).

\[ C(u,v)=\big(u^{-\theta}+v^{-\theta}-1\big)^{-1/\theta},\qquad c(u,v)=(1+\theta)(uv)^{-\theta-1}\big(u^{-\theta}+v^{-\theta}-1\big)^{-2-1/\theta}. \] \[ h(u\mid v)=v^{-\theta-1}\big(u^{-\theta}+v^{-\theta}-1\big)^{-1-1/\theta},\quad h^{-1}(w\mid v)=\Big[\big(w\,v^{\theta+1}\big)^{-\frac{\theta}{\theta+1}}+1-v^{-\theta}\Big]^{-1/\theta}. \] \[ \theta=\frac{2\tau}{1-\tau},\qquad \lambda_L=2^{-1/\theta},\qquad \lambda_U=0. \]

Gumbel — upper tail

\(\varphi(t)=(-\ln t)^\theta,\ \theta\ge1\). Let \(A=(-\ln u)^\theta+(-\ln v)^\theta\).

\[ C(u,v)=\exp\!\big(-A^{1/\theta}\big),\qquad c(u,v)=C(u,v)\,\frac{\big[(-\ln u)(-\ln v)\big]^{\theta-1}}{uv}\,A^{\frac1\theta-2}\big(A^{1/\theta}+\theta-1\big). \] \[ h(u\mid v)=C(u,v)\,A^{\frac1\theta-1}\,\frac{(-\ln v)^{\theta-1}}{v}\quad(\text{invert numerically}),\qquad \theta=\frac{1}{1-\tau},\ \ \lambda_U=2-2^{1/\theta},\ \ \lambda_L=0. \]

Frank — symmetric, both signs, no tail

\(\varphi(t)=-\ln\dfrac{e^{-\theta t}-1}{e^{-\theta}-1},\ \theta\in\mathbb R\setminus\{0\}\).

\[ C(u,v)=-\frac1\theta\ln\!\left(1+\frac{(e^{-\theta u}-1)(e^{-\theta v}-1)}{e^{-\theta}-1}\right),\qquad c(u,v)=\frac{\theta\,(1-e^{-\theta})\,e^{-\theta(u+v)}}{\big[(1-e^{-\theta})-(1-e^{-\theta u})(1-e^{-\theta v})\big]^2}. \] \[ h(u\mid v)=\frac{e^{-\theta v}(e^{-\theta u}-1)}{(e^{-\theta u}-1)(e^{-\theta v}-1)+(e^{-\theta}-1)},\qquad \tau=1-\frac{4}{\theta}\big[1-D_1(\theta)\big],\quad \lambda_L=\lambda_U=0, \] where \(D_1(\theta)=\tfrac1\theta\int_0^\theta \tfrac{t}{e^t-1}\,dt\) is the Debye function (invert \(\tau\!\to\!\theta\) numerically). \(\theta<0\) gives negative dependence, \(\theta>0\) positive — Frank is comprehensive, spanning \(\tau\in(-1,1)\) with no rotation.

Rotations

For a base copula \(C\) with density \(c\), the rotations move the tail/negate the dependence:

rotationCDFdensityeffect
180° (survival)\(u+v-1+C(1-u,1-v)\)\(c(1-u,1-v)\)\(\tau\) unchanged; \(\lambda_L\!\leftrightarrow\!\lambda_U\)
90°\(v-C(1-u,v)\)\(c(1-u,v)\)\(\tau\to-\tau\)
270°\(u-C(u,1-v)\)\(c(u,1-v)\)\(\tau\to-\tau\)

So can \(\tau\) reach \(-1\)? Yes — \(\tau\in[-1,1]\) for any copula; the endpoints are the comonotone \(M\) / countermonotone \(W\) copulas. Gaussian, t, and Frank span both signs directly (\(\theta\to\pm\infty\), \(\rho\to\pm1\)). Clayton and Gumbel are one-tailed positive-dependence families — their base only covers \(\tau\ge0\); a 90°/270° rotation gives the negative-dependence versions. Gumbel has no negative base at all.

4 · Many variables — regular vines

For \(d\ge3\) you cannot specify all pairwise copulas independently: they over-constrain (compatibility / positive-definiteness) and under-determine (many \(d\)-copulas share the same bivariate margins). The resolution is a pair-copula construction — factor the density into bivariate pieces, some conditional.

The recursion

Chain-rule the joint density and express each conditional with one pair-copula. With \(\mathbf v_{-j}=\mathbf v\setminus v_j\): \[ f(x\mid\mathbf v)=c_{x,v_j\mid\mathbf v_{-j}}\!\Big(F(x\mid\mathbf v_{-j}),\,F(v_j\mid\mathbf v_{-j})\Big)\cdot f(x\mid\mathbf v_{-j}), \] and the conditional CDFs propagate through the same h-functions: \[ F(x\mid\mathbf v)=h_{x,v_j;\mathbf v_{-j}}\!\Big(F(x\mid\mathbf v_{-j})\,\big|\,F(v_j\mid\mathbf v_{-j})\Big). \] Everything is built from the bivariate \(h\) and \(h^{-1}\) above.

R-vine density

A regular vine is a nested set of trees \(\mathcal V=(T_1,\dots,T_{d-1})\): \(T_1\) spans the \(d\) variables (\(d-1\) edges), and the edges of \(T_{m-1}\) become the nodes of \(T_m\) (proximity condition). Each edge \(e\) carries a pair-copula \(c_{j(e),k(e)\mid D(e)}\) with conditioned pair \(\{j,k\}\) and conditioning set \(D(e)\). There are exactly \(\sum_{m=1}^{d-1}(d-m)=\binom{d}{2}\) of them, and \[ f(\mathbf x)=\underbrace{\prod_{k=1}^{d} f_k(x_k)}_{\text{margins}}\ \cdot\!\!\prod_{m=1}^{d-1}\ \prod_{e\in E_m}\! c_{j(e),k(e)\mid D(e)}\!\Big(F(x_{j(e)}\mid\mathbf x_{D(e)}),\,F(x_{k(e)}\mid\mathbf x_{D(e)})\Big). \] Because it is a product of algebraically-free pieces, every choice of pair-copulas yields a valid joint — no positive-definiteness constraint, unlike a raw correlation matrix.

Simulation, simplification, truncation

  • Simulate by the inverse-h recursion: draw \(w_1,\dots,w_d\sim U(0,1)\) and invert down the trees (the \(d=3\) case: \(x_2=h^{-1}(w_2\mid x_1),\ x_3=h^{-1}\!\big(h^{-1}(w_3\mid h(x_1\mid x_2))\big|x_2\big)\)).
  • Simplified vine assumption: \(c_{j,k\mid D}\) depends on \(D\) only as a set, not on the value \(\mathbf x_D\). Dropping it gives the fully general (non-simplified) vine — dense in all copulas.
  • Truncate at level \(K\): set \(c_{\cdot\mid D}=\Pi\) (independence) for all trees \(T_{K+1..d-1}\). Parameters drop from \(\binom{d}{2}\) to \(\sum_{m=1}^{K}(d-m)\); usually a couple of trees capture the dominant dependence.
  • Structure selection (Dißmann): build each tree as a maximum spanning tree weighted by \(|\hat\tau_{jk\mid D}|\), fit the best pair-copula per edge by AIC, recurse.

Running example — LaLonde job training

The covariate block is \(d=8\): {age, educ, black, hisp, married, nodegree, re74, re75}. A full R-vine is \(\binom{8}{2}=28\) pair-copulas. In practice:

  • \(T_1\) is a spanning tree over the 8 covariates. The dominant \(|\tau|\) edges are the earnings-history persistence re74 — re75 and the mechanical educ — nodegree; these anchor the tree.
  • Prior earnings re74,re75 are right-skewed with a mass at 0 → asymmetric-tail pair-copulas (Clayton/Gumbel or their rotations), not Gaussian.
  • The binaries {black, hisp, married, nodegree} need the discrete-margin treatment (§5).
  • Truncating at \(K=2\) (≈ 13 pair-copulas) captures most dependence; the rest \(\approx\Pi\). The tree structure can be selected (Dißmann) or read off the confounding DAG — a bidirected edge among covariates is one pair-copula.

This is the honest answer to "eventually there are lots of variables": the atomic bivariate cell never changes — you arrange \(\binom{d}{2}\) of them along a vine, truncate the tail trees to independence, and let structure selection (or the causal graph) decide which pairs are direct. The editor's 3-variable case is this with \(d=3\).

5 · Discrete margins

For discrete \(F_k\), Sklar gives existence but the copula is not unique off the grid \(\mathrm{Ran}(F_j)\times\mathrm{Ran}(F_k)\), and attainable dependence is squeezed by the discrete Fréchet–Hoeffding bounds — you cannot impose arbitrary correlation between two rare binaries. For binaries with means \(p,q\) the correlation is capped: \[ \rho \;\le\; \frac{\min(p,q)-pq}{\sqrt{pq(1-p)(1-q)}}. \] The practical generator is the latent-Gaussian threshold (NORTA): draw from a continuous copula and threshold to the discrete margin. This is what the engine's copula_marginal node already does, and why "set any correlation" is a lie for the binary covariates in LaLonde.
