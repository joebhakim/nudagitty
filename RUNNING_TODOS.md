- [x] Brown et al. K562 intervention-mechanics explorer: replace the current faithful full-network viewer as the primary experience. Keep the full graph available as context, but make the main task "pick a perturbation gene and inspect predicted downstream causal mechanics."
  - Product stance: Brown et al. is not primarily a gene-by-gene perturbation showcase. Replogle et al. generated the Perturb-seq intervention data; Brown et al. reanalyze it to estimate an all-pairs ACE matrix `R_hat`, infer a sparse direct network `G_hat`, and study global topology, paths, centrality, and validation. The UI must distinguish "paper-reported aggregate result" from "Nudagitty-derived inspection of the released estimates."
  - Literature context to preserve in copy: true reusable gene-level perturbation examples are still sparse. Key experimental anchors include Perturb-seq / CRISP-seq / CROP-seq, Norman combinatorial CRISPRa, Replogle genome-scale Perturb-seq, and Frangieh Perturb-CITE-seq. Prediction papers such as GEARS and PDGrapher are relevant, but recent benchmarks warn that unseen perturbation prediction often captures systematic perturbed-vs-control variation rather than perturbation-specific biology.
  - Source/provenance labels: every row or card should be tagged as one of `reported in paper`, `released Brown estimate`, or `Nudagitty derived from released estimates`. Do not imply Brown et al. highlighted a specific gene pair unless the paper text does so.
  - Primary screen: a perturbation picker. Selecting `RPS3`, `HSPA9`, `PHB`, `MED10`, `DYNLL1`, etc. shows: guide knockdown/effectiveness metadata, count of significant total effects, count of nonzero direct edges, count of significant direct edges, count of significant mediated/non-edge effects, and a "blast radius" distribution.
  - Intervention scenario: add a slider for `do(gene += delta SD)`. Default can use the observed guide knockdown from Supplementary Data 2 when available, but it must be marked as a derived scenario. Forecast downstream change as `delta * R_hat`, with uncertainty/FDR from the released table.
  - Downstream effects table: for the selected exposure gene, rank outcomes by absolute predicted change or absolute `R_hat`. Columns should include outcome gene, predicted change, `R_hat`, SE, FDR, direct `G_hat`, path length, shortest-path effect, effect explained, and sign agreement between direct and total effect.
  - Mechanics cards: automatically surface examples of (1) direct edge and total effect agree, (2) significant total effect with no direct sparse edge, (3) direct and total signs disagree, (4) shortest path explains little of the total effect, and (5) shortest path exceeds 100%, meaning other paths cancel part of that path's effect.
  - Graph surface: default to selected-gene subgraphs, not the 788-node hairball. Provide toggles for `total effects`, `direct G_hat edges`, and `shortest-path explanation for selected pair`. The full graph should be a secondary "all released edges" view.
  - Calibration anchors from the paper: show 788 genes, 10,423 nonzero `G_hat` edges, 131,943 FDR 5% ACEs including self-effects, 47.5% connected ordered pairs, median connected path length about 2.67, median FDR-significant path length about 2.46, median shortest-path effect explained 11.14%, and 5,448 FDR-significant pairs above 100% shortest-path effect explained.
  - Biological interpretation: include centrality/essentiality/heredity panels only as secondary context. Useful contrasts include "high outdegree is not the same as high intervention blast radius" and "centrality can mark constrained/essential genes, but this is network biology rather than validated therapeutic targeting."
  - Data model: added a compact per-exposure index of released `R_hat` rows, especially top FDR-significant total effects and examples where `G_hat = 0`.
  - Validation: added a browser test that selects known genes, verifies paper anchor counts, verifies mediated-only/sign-disagreement examples, and verifies direct-edge/full-graph/total-effect mode switching.
  - Out of scope for this pass: claiming experimental validation of specific gene-pair stories, predicting unseen perturbations beyond Brown's released estimates, or turning the inferred cyclic network into a clean small DAG.
